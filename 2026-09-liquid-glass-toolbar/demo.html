Liquid glass toolbar

One glass surface that changes shape. Six things worth looking at, in order: what the bar is made of, how a label opens, what the panel does to the surface, how the motion is staged, the five materials, and what happens when the box it sits in gets too narrow.

01 At rest — click through it

Five actions, and they are not evenly spaced. Four cluster together and the fifth is pushed to the far end, which is what makes a closed bar read as a toolbar rather than as a row of buttons — the gap says the last item is a different kind of thing before any label appears to explain it.

Exactly one item carries a label, and it is the one under the lit lozenge: a label here is the selection indicator, not a tooltip. The state it reads is the ARIA the component needs anyway — aria-current on the three destinations, aria-expanded on the panel button — so component.js writes two attributes and nothing else. It never measures, positions or animates, and there is no sliding element: the outgoing pill shrinks back to a square while the incoming one grows, and because they are adjacent the highlight reads as one mass travelling.

02 The reveal — one mechanism, two payloads

Every morph in the bar is the same grid column going 0fr to 1fr with overflow: hidden over it. What sits in that track is a text label on four of the items and a text input on the fifth, and the track does not know which — which is why the search field and the nav labels read as the same piece of surface moving rather than as two separate effects.

The catch is that a box cannot be narrower than its own padding, so the gap between icon and label is text-indent: an indent is part of the line, disappears with the text, and still counts towards the width the open track measures. Padded, every collapsed item kept an 11px stripe of nothing beside it. The input is the one payload measured in a length instead of 1fr, because a text input has no meaningful max-content width.

This state is pinned open with --searching, an outside handle for callers that cannot synthesise the event. Click the toolbar and the pin drops: from there the field runs on :focus-within and needs no script at all.

03 Activity — the extended view

Opening the panel does not slide a second sheet out from under a pill. There is one surface: the bar and the panel live inside the same shell, and that shell is the only thing carrying a backdrop filter, a rim and a shadow. What changes is its shape — the panel row goes 0fr to 1fr, its width 0 to 30rem, and the surface re-measures around it because it is width: fit-content.

The corner is the one thing that does not change. --radius-open is pad + hit / 2 — exactly the radius that runs concentric with the corner actions inside it — and closed, 999px clamps to half the bar’s height and lands on the same number. So the height does all the work, and the inner pills never look like they are sitting in a box cut to a different curve.

It is called Activity rather than Notifications on purpose. Notifications is a promise about delivery; what these four rows are is a log — somebody commented, a build finished, a review was asked for, an invoice cleared. Naming it for the content also stops the component being read as an alert centre, which is a different thing with different rules about badges and dismissal.

04 Motion — two clocks, no overshoot

Everything here animates layout — a track, a width, a radius, a surface measuring itself — so none of it can be moved onto a transform without losing the thing that makes it read as one surface breathing. The smoothness had to come from the curve instead.

No overshoot, and that is the important one. A curve that passes 1 and settles back is lovely on a transform; on a width it means the bar grows past its final size and comes back, and with the surface centred the whole thing slides out and in. That is a wobble, not a bounce. Two other savings came from the same round: the lit lozenge lost a nested backdrop-filter — a backdrop filter inside a backdrop-filtered element takes a second snapshot, and it sat on the one element that resizes every frame — and the blur came down, first from 20px to 14px and then to 8px when the materials were thinned, because backdrop blur costs by radius and re-runs whenever the surface changes size.

Press Activity below and watch the order: the label arrives first and the card follows it down. That staging is free — it is what two durations give you.

--reveal · 220ms
The label track and the pill’s trailing pad. About 60px of travel.
--morph · 300ms
The panel row, the panel width and the corner. About ten times the distance, so it gets its own clock.
--fade · 140ms + 60ms in
The feed’s opacity. The delay is declared in the open state only, so closing snaps the text out instead of leaving it hanging in a collapsed box.

05 Materials — five palettes, one construction

Nothing below the variable block changes for any of these. A material is a tint, a filter, a rim, a sheen, an edge, a drop, a lozenge, an ink and an accent — and each is a whole palette rather than a hue rotation, because the ink has to move with the tint. All five sit on Home, deliberately: a set of materials is only comparable if it is the same state five times.

They are all thinner than they were. The first version carried the colour in the tint at 42–64% opacity, and a sheet that opaque is a coloured surface with a texture underneath rather than glass — nothing of the page survived the trip through it. So the tint came down to 28–44%, the work moved into the filter — brightness now does the darkening the tint used to do, contrast keeps the range from flattening — and the blur came down with it, because blur is frost: past about 10px the ground stops being a place and becomes a wash. What you should be able to see on every sheet now is the page’s own cross-light continuing across it.

Crystal is that argument taken to the end, and the reason the recipe grew two values. At 7% it has almost no body, so it is carried entirely by what it does to the light and by its edges: a rim at 0.74, a white sheen, and a second hairline along the inside of the bottom — light that went through the sheet and came back off the far face. That one is the cheapest available stand-in for refraction, which CSS cannot do, and it is most of what gives a pane this thin any thickness at all. It also takes brightness slightly below 1: real glass absorbs a little, and a sheet a shade darker than the page with bright edges reads as glass, where a brighter one reads as paint.

What thinning costs is contrast, and the label pays it. White type on moss was 2.3:1 against the lit pill before and is about 4:1 now — better, because the glass got darker and the lozenge became a rim rather than a fill, and still the number to watch if you re-tint this. Crystal’s dark ink sits near 11:1, since a clear sheet leaves the ink standing on the page. The other cost is independence: moss on a flat white page is still moss; crystal on a flat white page is a rim and a shadow with nothing inside them.

06 Narrow — a container query, not a media query

Five targets at 48px, four gaps and the surface’s own padding need about 264px. Below that the row cannot fit, and because the surface clips, what went missing was the trailing action — the component quietly lost its last item rather than getting tight. Nothing squeezes its way out of that: the actions hold a min-width so they stay tappable, and their padding is not a flex length, so an action forced narrower just spills its icon into its neighbour.

So it scales instead. One breakpoint, and it moves only the values the rest of the file is already derived from, which is why this is the same component at 40px targets rather than a second small one. The box below is 15rem wide; the query reads that box and not the window, so the full-size bar further up the page is unaffected — a component that reads the viewport is wrong the moment it lands in a sidebar.

At this width there is no room for a label at all, so the reveal stays shut and the lit pill is the only thing saying which item you are on. That is the right thing to lose: the label was never the selection indicator’s only channel.
